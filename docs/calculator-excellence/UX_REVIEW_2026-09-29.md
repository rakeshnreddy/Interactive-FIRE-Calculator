## The intended calculator experience

1. A short question, scope and clear input panel; no long marketing introduction.
2. Show the meaningful base inputs. Required fields never hide in optional groups.
3. Directly under the panel heading, a visible **Customize this estimate** link with a brief benefit (“Add extra payments, housing costs or future changes”). This opens/focuses the real controls; it does not toggle an unrelated panel.
4. Optional groups named by the job: **Pay extra**, **Include housing costs**, **Timing and frequency**, **Fees and purchasing power**, **Future changes**. Summary shows active count and entered values, or an honest unset/omitted state. Display preferences are separate.
5. One dominant answer with currency, time basis, sample/edited state and the top model limitation. Two or three supporting metrics earn their place. An answer can be “not enough information” or “no payoff under these inputs”.
6. A matched visual explaining the answer, backed by the exact same schedule/data. No chart for a one-number utility unless it helps the decision. Show the biggest editable driver and one relevant comparison.
7. A clear optional next step: keep the decision, compare it or save a dated baseline for a future review. Save does not imply an account was needed to calculate. Numeric details, audit table, methodology and education follow.

Do not give every route every control. A mortgage needs ownership costs and payment timing; a SIP needs deposit rhythm and contribution growth; GST needs tax direction; a return calculator needs dated cash flows or an honest endpoint scope. Each of the 83 rows specifies the appropriate direction. Add new controls only when they have an implemented mathematical effect, test, limitation and clear default. Optional neutral 0 applies to additive fees/events, not universally to interest, required rates or contractual withdrawal conventions. FIRE rates remain empty and required; examples require explicit action; explicit zero remains valid.

## Landing redesign contract

Primary positioning: **“Make your next money decision clearer.”** Supporting copy proposal: “Work out what to save, what borrowing costs, or what retirement needs. Change the assumptions and see why the answer moves.” Trust line: “Free calculators. No account needed. Estimates, not promises.” Test the language; do not assert it converts better before user evidence.

Desktop order: navigation → concise headline/one primary FIRE-planning action plus an obvious calculator-search secondary → small truthful decision example → three question paths → the calculation/compare/save/review loop → evidence of scope/privacy → brief FAQ. Mobile order: headline → primary action/search → three question paths → optional example detail. The example must no longer push non-retirement choices 1.7k pixels down.

Keep no more than three first-layer choices: **Plan retirement**, **Pay down debt**, **Reach a savings goal**. Mortgage/EMI/SIP are search suggestions or meaningful links in those paths, not a second duplicate first-layer nav. Preserve stable URLs, real anchors, back behavior and US/India labels. Retain library search and task groupings; remove breadth-as-value (“83 calculators”) from the main value proposition rather than pretending the catalogue shrank. Public header account creation is secondary to starting a calculation.

The illustration is already useful and theme-aware. Simplify the first view: one question, one result, a brief entered-assumption line and a supported driver. Keep its real engine data and “illustrative example” badge. No unsourced decorative up-only growth curve, fake progress or substituted currency symbols. If adding an INR example, use an independently labeled fixture and recalculate it; currency presentation is not FX conversion. Keep all explanatory accuracy available under a real disclosure.

This palette is a proposal, **not an implemented or globally contrast-certified theme**. B46 measures text and controls against actual composited backgrounds. Do not use the muted ink for small text on arbitrary tinted surfaces. Keep a restrained teal-to-cobalt gradient in the hero atmosphere or one primary action; do not put gradient fills or translucent glass behind dense money data. Glass is useful in navigation/overlays, with solid fallback and reduced-transparency/forced-colors behavior. Light and dark must feel deliberately composed rather than the same translucent panel with inverted text. State, hierarchy, labels, borders and shapes carry meaning without color. No pulsing nudge, forced tour, hover-only control or dismiss-once pressure badge.

## Acquisition, habit and revenue

Start with people actively revisiting a retirement or savings plan. Public borrowing and tax tools can acquire users, but do not force every visitor into FIRE. Match the next saved decision to their actual task. The recurring job is **“What changed since my last review, and do I need to adjust the plan?”** The monthly review should retain the original assumptions/date, current balance, one meaningful change and the user's keep/revise decision.

Focus distribution on five polished entry journeys: FIRE, mortgage/extra payment, SIP, savings goal and emergency fund. Publish one sourced worked decision guide per journey, with a real calculator, clear authorship/review date and limitations. Use owned-domain canonicals and Search Console after production setup, monitor actual impressions/queries and link correctness. Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) supports useful original explanations rather than mass near-duplicate pages. Preserve variant URLs and avoid unfounded ranking or traffic guarantees. Do not add SEO/growth language to visible product copy.

Before paid campaigns, run owner-recruited usability sessions with 5 US and 5 India participants. Proposed, unproven gates: at least 8/10 notice and use relevant customization without being told where it is; 8/10 identify sample versus personal result and correctly explain the main limitation; no participant mistakes interest-only principal, APY or omitted housing costs. Record actual failures and cohort sizes, not a fabricated % PASS. Then recruit 20 real plan creators and observe at least 6 meaningful returns within 45 days before opening B13's no-charge price study. This directional pilot is small and not a statistical market estimate.

North star: genuine users completing a dated saved-plan review at least seven days after their baseline, with an explicit keep/revise decision. Funnel: started → valid result → useful customization/comparison → saved decision → balance/profile context → returning completed review. Reuse the existing consented, allowlisted analytics architecture; no amounts, rates, balances, plan IDs, cash-flow rows or screenshots in events, no session replay, no identifiers in URLs beyond the existing privacy-reviewed contract. Task B51 specifies denominators and exclusions for synthetic users, workers and owner checks.

Revenue recommendation remains one primary subscription after evidence: charge for continued decision management (multiple scenarios, history/comparisons, useful reports and later permissioned collaboration), while calculators, basic explanations and privacy/export/deletion remain accessible. Test **$48 versus $72/year US; ₹999 versus ₹1499/year India** as hypotheses, not approved prices or demand. One optional secondary paid report can be tested later; ads and undisclosed affiliate influence are poor first-year trust tradeoffs. Never paywall correcting a calculation or retrieving/deleting personal data. B13 remains locked until real retention evidence; B14 mobile work remains locked. Payment entity/country, taxes, cancellation/refund terms and support capacity precede any checkout.
